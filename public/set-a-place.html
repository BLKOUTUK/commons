Why we're setting a table

Sometimes loneliness and isolation can feel like a punishment for being who you are, meted out all over again.

Sometimes we're embarrassed to ask, or to invite those who are alone.

Or we can't face another argument with those still learning to love us for us.

Sometimes love is thicker than water.

So we're making our own welcome, the way Black queer people always have: the ballroom houses, the flats opened up to whoever had nowhere else to be, BLKOUT's own first brunch, forty of us in one room in 2019. Joseph Beam Day carries it on.

Monday 28 December · central Croydon

A holiday nobody has to edit themselves for.

1pm · Top floor

Ivor's Table

A sit-down lunch named for Ivor Cummings, who grew up in Croydon and welcomed the Windrush passengers in 1948. Served by BLKOUT's board and friends. Jason Jones, fresh from winning his case at the Privy Council, says a few words.

Afternoon · Ground floor

The Play Parlour

Black men at play. An afternoon among new and old friends, getting creative: make art, try out new talents, join the games. Step into the Choose Joy crew's new media installation and put yourself on the cover of BLKOUT Review. Every guest takes home an Ivor's Compass journal.

7pm

Our King's Speech

Monologues by and for Black queer men, written by the Choose Joy crew, setting our agenda for the new year.

Evening · Middle floor

Club Frolic

Our White Party: cabaret, then we dance the year out. It's also the day BLKOUT's membership opens to everyone.

How we look after guests

Hosted, not handled.

  • Some guests are younger men, some are elders, and some have come here seeking sanctuary. They come because a partner they trust invited them.
  • No labels, no cameras unless you say yes, and a buddy at the door. Guests seeking sanctuary are never photographed.
  • A named safeguarding lead all day, a quiet room, and a code of conduct every volunteer signs. 18+.
  • Donor details are kept separate from guest details, always.

Good to know

Who is BLKOUT?

BLKOUT is the Black Queer Men's Liberation Collective. We host gatherings, build space for dialogue, and create community-owned technology for the health, wealth and happiness of Black gay, bi and trans men across the UK.

Is my gift tax-deductible?

BLKOUT Creative Ltd is a community benefit society (FCA RS009639), not a registered charity, so gifts aren't eligible for Gift Aid. Every pound still goes to the day.

Why Zeffy?

Zeffy charges us no platform or card fees, so 100% of your gift reaches the day. At checkout Zeffy may suggest an optional tip to Zeffy itself; you can set it to £0.

Why 28 December?

Joseph Beam edited In the Life and wrote that Black men loving Black men is the revolutionary act. He died on 27 December 1988 and was born on 30 December. The days between Christmas and New Year are ours to fill. Joseph Beam Day is the first of BLKOUT's Rites, hosted by a crew of local men.

When do thank-yous arrive?

Digital thank-yous are emailed in January. T-shirts are posted in January (UK), or you can collect yours on the day. Gratitude-wall names go up on the day.

Can I give in another way?

Write to rob@blkoutuk.com and we'll find a way that works for you, whether that's a bank transfer, a gift in kind or your time on the day.
