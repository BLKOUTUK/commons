The Dancefloor

Thursday 6 August · 10 slides · post in this order

Caption

Nobody funds the dancefloor. It is where most of us were first held anyway.

Before the counselling service, before the helpline, before anyone wrote a strategy about isolation — there was a room, a promoter carrying the financial risk, and someone on the door who knew your name. Club nights are not the light relief in this list. For many of us they were the entire support infrastructure, run on ticket money and nerve.

Day two of LGBTQ+ Global Majority Week.

HERE
Club Kali — founded 1995 by DJ Ritu and Rita, a youth worker and a domestic violence worker doing this in their own time. Thirty years of Bollywood, bhangra and belonging. clubkali.com
Chakula at the Africa Centre — Black and African LGBTQ+ nights, curated by Vee Belinga. A QTIPOC-led programme inside a bigger institution is still ours.
Sunday Happy Day — @yveelicious
The CookOUT — queer African collective lifting Black British LGBTQ+ culture. thecookoutparty.com
Coven — five floors of the old G-A-Y Bar on Old Compton Street, the first new LGBTQ+ club on that street in over a decade. Started as a night in Hackney Wick last year.

ELSEWHERE
BBB, Black Blanc Beur — every Sunday in Paris for twenty-five years. It came from the Kelma association, founded because there was nowhere for gay Arabs to go: the racism ran through the gay scene too.
Liquorice, Paris — Afro-Black queer nights, and hosts of Global Black Pride's Paris launch.
Gayhane, Berlin — Turkish and Arabic queer Kreuzberg at SO36, every fourth Saturday for fifteen years.

AND NOT ALL OF IT IS A NIGHTCLUB
Stray Cats, Glasgow — LGBTQ+ people of colour, first Sunday of the month, papercraft and company.
Queer Mehndi Night, Birmingham — henna, and a monthly ritual to gather, connect and rest.

Sanctuary and Rights

Friday 7 August · 9 slides · post in this order

Caption

The distance between white gay men and trans women was the gap the religious right walked straight through. Wedges work because we let the space open.

So sanctuary is not only shelter. It is refusing the wedge — including the oldest one, the demand that we choose between our faith and ourselves.

Day three of LGBTQ+ Global Majority Week.

HERE
Imaan — the UK's oldest LGBTQI Muslim charity, founded 1999, still volunteer-led. A quarter of a century of a conversation the rest of us were told could not be had. imaan.org.uk
House of Rainbow — Rev Jide Macaulay's affirming Christian fellowship, writing witness statements for people in detention while it is at it. houseofrainbow.org
African Rainbow Family — twelve years this year. LGBTIQ+ people of African heritage through an asylum system built to disbelieve them. africanrainbowfamily.org

ELSEWHERE
LGBT+ Rights Ghana — founded in Accra in 2018, still organising through a political climate designed to make it impossible. Read what they post, then think about what we call brave here.
ECADE — thirty-one organisations across nine Eastern Caribbean countries, Virgin Islands to Grenada. Colonial buggery laws have fallen in five of them. ecequality.org
ANTRA, Brazil — maps the murders of travestis and trans people, because a state that will not count you can claim you were never there. antrabrasil.org
Haus of Khameleon, Fiji — young trans Fijians organising across rights, land and climate together, because for them those were never separate files. hausofkhameleon.com

Also: Hidayah · BME PROUD · Rainbows Across Borders · LASS Sheffield

Stage, Screen and Page

Saturday 8 August · 9 slides · post in this order

Caption

We say representation matters. We rarely ask: to whom, in front of which audience, and on whose terms?

Here is our version. Culture is what we do, not simply who we are — which makes the making the change itself, not the warm-up for it.

Day four of LGBTQ+ Global Majority Week.

HERE
Ajamu Studios — four decades photographing the Black male body, same-sex desire and the darkroom, under a banner he calls pleasure activism. Not survival, not struggle, not evidence for a funder. Pleasure. Co-founded the rukus! archive in 2005. ajamu-studio.com
The Common Press — London's first consciously queer intersectional bookshop, run by queer BIPOC, trans and non-binary people and queer women, now across two East London sites. Seven thousand titles, fifteen hundred on QTIPOC experience. commonpress.co.uk
Elevate Studios — Blaise Singh's media facility in Hounslow. Queer Global documents QTIPOC and migrant Londoners. Commercial work pays for the free work. elevatecommunityproductions.org
House of Suarez — Darren Suarez's vogue house, Liverpool since 2006, and the Vogue Ball across Liverpool and Manchester. He started Liverpool is Burning in 2008. UK ballroom did not arrive from America last year.
Our Space Leeds — QTIPoC art and music, with project profits going to local gender-affirmation fundraisers. The economics are the politics. ourspaceleeds.com

ELSEWHERE
The Queer Muslim Project, India — Asia's leading digital and cultural platform for queer, Muslim and diverse voices. tqmp.in
Tonga Leitis' Association — founded 1992 by Joey Joleen Mataele, and behind the Miss Galaxy pageant. Culture carrying the argument rather than decorating it. tongaleiti.org

The Network

Sunday 9 August · 11 slides · post in this order

Caption

Critical mass is inert without critical connections.

A thousand of us in one room changes nothing. What moves is the wide bridge — overlapping ties across difference, built by people who kept turning up and putting the chairs out. So the last day of this week is not organisations. It is the lines between them.

HERE — the DiaspoRainbow network
Glitter Cymru in Cardiff, whose week this is. Bradford Black and Brown Queers, who ran Queer Joy in 2025, Bradford's first ever QTIPOC festival, For Us By Us, made because the city had none. Radical Rhizomes in Brighton, 300 members, free at the door, food included. Rainbow Noir in Manchester, open since 2012. Bristol QPOC Socials, and a QTIPOC Inter-City Gathering on 11 September. Stray Cats in Glasgow. Queer Mehndi Night in Birmingham. Black Thrive Lambeth and the Black Boy Joy Club in London.

Read that list again and notice what it is not. Not London with a few outposts. Cardiff, Bradford, Bristol, Glasgow, Manchester, Birmingham, Brighton — a national network that decided being in touch with each other was itself the work, while the sector held another panel about hard-to-reach communities.

Also worth your follow: Queertopia Consortium, non-binary led and founded in 2024. Melanin Vybz, with LGBTQ+ people of colour aged 55 and over — still the only UK group we can find doing it. Bare Radicals.

ELSEWHERE
QAYN — queer, feminist and lesbian-led across West and Central Africa since 2010. qayn.org
ISDAO — a West African activist fund resourcing West African organising. Money that does not cross an ocean to reach us. isdao.org
LooM SWANA — digital rights and security, so queer and trans organising across South West Asia and North Africa survives being watched. loomswana.org
Global Black Pride — Paris, 9 to 13 September. It grew out of Global Black Gay Men Connect, which BLKOUT helped found. Not spectators at this one. globalblackpride.org

Forty names, and we have barely started. That is not a gap in the list. That is the size of us.
