BLKOUT Annual Picnic · Sunday 16 August · Regent's Park · 1–7.30pm

The useful bits.

Everything you might wonder before Sunday. None of it complicated — it's a picnic.

Do I need a ticket?

No. Free, always. Everything you need is on this page — nothing is held back for a list. Saving a spot just helps us plan the food, and puts the pin and a weather word straight in your inbox.

Where exactly in Regent's Park?

The exact corner gets picked late — the weather and the ground decide it. It lands right here on Friday 14 August: a what3words pin, the nearest gates, step-free routes and toilets. Sign-ups get the same by email. No secrets — just a park that needs a look at the sky first.

What should I bring?

A blanket or rug, food you love, your people, your ease. We sit low on the grass, the way a picnic should be — rugs beat tables and chairs, and gazebos can stay home. Travel light; the park does the décor.

If sitting low doesn't work for your body, bring the chair you need. Comfort is always welcome.

Can I come on my own?

Yes — that's the whole point. Bring yourself. Make a crew there. There'll be games, food being passed round, and people who are looking forward to meeting you. Whether you've been to everything or you've never come to anything, this is your invitation.

Food and drink — what's the deal?

Bring what you love, enough to share if you can — a plate passed round is half the day. Bring it ready to eat: no barbecues or cooking in the park. And nobody goes hungry — there's backup food on us.

Drinks are welcome, including something to share — free to friends, and 18+ for anything stronger than lemonade. Cans and plastic beat glass: bare feet on grass, all afternoon.

What about music?

Crews bring their own sound — one small speaker per crew, the kind you carry in one hand, kept to your own rugs. We let the afternoon breathe first: conversation, games and food until 5, then the music comes up with the golden hour.

Voices are welcome all day. If you sing, sing.

Kids? Dogs? Elders?

All of us, every age. Kids welcome. Elders honoured. Dogs on their best behaviour — and on a lead if the park asks. This is the family picnic, in the fullest sense of the word.

Is it accessible?

We choose the spot with this in mind — flat grass near step-free paths, public toilets in reach. The Friday location drop will carry the practical detail, here and by email. If there's an access need you'd like us to think about, email rob@blkoutuk.com and a human will think it through with you.

What if it rains?

We watch the sky all week and post a weather word right here before the day — sign-ups get it by email too. Light rain means umbrellas and bravado; proper rain means the plan appears in the same places. London usually behaves in August. Usually.

What's the chip-in?

Coming is free, always. If you can, chip in on the day — it covers the food and the sound. Nobody will shake a bucket at you on the grass.

We're also collecting for queer people in Accra organising under Ghana's new anti-LGBTQ bill — three years for being who you are, ten for funding anyone who is. Give what you'd have spent on a round →

When does it end — and what's after?

The picnic winds down at 7.30 as the light goes. Then: karaoke. Zodiac Bar, 119 Hampstead Road — a short walk from the park's east side. 8–11pm, free, all welcome. Come sing, or come watch people feel things.

How do I find everyone on the day?

Follow the pin, then follow the sound of a good time. We're the constellation of picnic blankets. If your phone dies, ask the park for the most joy per square metre and walk towards it.

Can I help?

Gladly. Extra hands for welcome, setup and pack-down are gold — reply to your spot confirmation email, or email rob@blkoutuk.com and say so. Two hours of helping is a very good way to arrive alone and leave with a crew.
